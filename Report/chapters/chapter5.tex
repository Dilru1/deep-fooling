\section{Conclusion and Future Work}

This study successfully developed and evaluated a 1D Convolutional Neural Network (1DCNN) architecture for the autonomous control of a vessel, comparing its performance against a standard Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) and a traditional Baseline controller. Our development of the 1DCNN focused on leveraging convolutional layers to enhance feature extraction from temporal environmental data, specifically targeting Course Made Good (CMG) optimization across diverse maritime conditions.

The results interpretation demonstrates that the 1DCNN is the most robust and high-performing model among those tested. It achieved a peak performance of 24.6 knots, notably under out-of-distribution high-wind conditions ($20\,\text{kts}$), which underscores its superior ability to extrapolate learned physical dynamics beyond the training envelope. While the MLP showed competitive results in moderate, in-distribution scenarios, it suffered from significant operational instability and policy divergence in complex heading-wind configurations, such as the $H:135^\circ$ case. Conversely, the 1DCNN maintained a stable and continuous performance profile, utilizing a learned "adaptive jitter" strategy to make high-frequency micro-corrections that maximized average speed. This indicates that the 1DCNN provides a more effective structural bias for maritime control tasks than traditional dense architectures.

Future work will focus on the following areas to further advance the system's capabilities:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dynamic Obstacle Avoidance:} Extending the 1DCNN policy to incorporate sensor fusion (e.g., LiDAR or Radar) for real-time navigation in environments with moving obstacles.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Objective Optimization:} Refining the reward function to balance maximum CMG with fuel efficiency and mechanical longevity by penalizing excessive rudder "jitter."
    \item \textbf{Transfer Learning for Variable Vessel Dynamics:} Investigating the portability of the learned 1DCNN weights to different hull designs and propulsion systems to determine the universality of the learned control laws.
    \item \textbf{Real-World Deployment:} Transitioning from high-fidelity simulations to hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) testing and eventually full-scale maritime trials to validate the 1DCNN's performance under stochastic real-world sea states.
\end{itemize}
